A few of our favorite places around Snogebæk — where to eat, and how to spend a slow day exploring the island. For even more ideas, the official Bornholm visitor guide is a great place to start.

Food

  • Søren’s VærtshusThe local pub and pizza joint, known for its festive vibes and sea-worthy decor.
  • Snogebæk RøgeriOur Sunday lunch buffet is here, but it’s almost certainly worth visiting twice :)
  • Daglig BrugsenThe only local, walkable grocery store, with a surprisingly good range of vegan dairy options.

Experiences

  • BeachBalka beach is a gentle crescent of soft white sand with shallow, calm water — an easy walk from Snogebæk and perfect for a swim or a slow morning by the sea. For famously powder-fine sand, the dunes at Dueodde are a short drive south.
  • BikingBornholm has an extensive cycling network around the coast and inland. In Snogebæk, Boss Cykler and AP Cykler rent bikes for around $15/day. For routes, the island’s cycling guide (PDF) maps scenic loops along the coast and through the countryside.
  • ThriftingThrift shops, vintage shops, and flea markets are common all over Denmark, and especially prolific on Bornholm. Alongside the brick-and-mortar establishments (PIF Genbrug, Fru Due, and the flea market at the Snogebæk harbor on Saturdays), you might occasionally pass thrift stalls — unmanned garage sales outside people’s houses — as you wander around. These run on an honor system and accept either MobilePay (a Danish version of Venmo) or cash. If you’re a second-hand connoisseur, we recommend a short, bikable trip to Nexø to check out larger establishments like KlonseKlovnen and Det Røde Kors (the Red Cross).
  • Viking & historic sitesHammershus, one of Northern Europe’s largest castle ruins, crowns the cliffs at the island’s northern tip — a scenic drive away and well worth it. Closer to the island’s medieval story are Bornholm’s round churches (rundkirker): whitewashed fortress-churches whose interiors, like Østerlars, still carry medieval wall paintings.
